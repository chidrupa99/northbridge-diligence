Screening Memo — Target Corporation (TGT)

Prepared for the Northbridge deal team · Source: SEC EDGAR · FY2025 basis · 29 July 2026
Neutral
Snapshot. Target (NYSE: TGT) is a general-merchandise retailer at scale and at a plateau. Revenue is flat over five years — $106.0B in FY2021 to $104.8B in FY2025, a −0.3% CAGR[S1,S3] — while operating income fell 43%, from $8,946M to $5,117M[S1,S3]. Leverage is 1.75x and interest coverage 11.5x[S1]. A margin problem inside a stable, well-capitalised business — not a solvency one.
The question: was FY2021's 8.4% operating margin the anomaly, or is FY2025's 4.9% the new normal?
Fiscal calendar. Target's year ends the Saturday closest to 31 January, so FY2025 ended 2026-01-31 and FY2023 ended 2024-02-03. Comparing against a December filer's like-numbered year introduces an eleven-month offset.

A shock followed by an incomplete recovery. FY2022 was the break — gross margin fell 470bp to 24.6%, operating margin more than halved, and free cash flow went negative (−$1,510M) as capex peaked at $5,528M against collapsed operating cash flow[S1,S2]. Gross margin has since recovered to 27.9%, within 140bp of FY2021.

Operating margin has not. It sits at 4.9% and has drifted down in each of the last three years (5.3% → 5.2% → 4.9%) even as gross margin held. Gross recovering while operating does not points at costs below the gross line rather than at pricing or mix — worth isolating before forming a view.

Risk signals — computed

Raised in code against fixed thresholds. Every high/medium flag appears here; none added or removed by hand.
High
LIQUIDITY — Current ratio is 0.94 (below the 1.0 floor); current liabilities exceed current assets. [S1]
Read as
Structural, not deteriorating. A sub-1.0 current ratio is normal for a large-format retailer — inventory turns fast and suppliers are paid on terms, so payables structurally exceed receivables. Target's ratio has sat between 0.91x and 0.99x in every year of the window[S1]. Against 11.5x coverage and $5.5B of cash, this is not a liquidity concern.

It is the clearest illustration of a stated limitation: thresholds are one global set. A 1.0x floor is right for a manufacturer and wrong for a retailer. The flag is correct — the rule fired as specified — but a deal lead should read it as an artefact of the threshold. Sector-relative bands are on the roadmap for exactly this.

No other flag fired. Specifically absent: NEGATIVE_EQUITY, EARNINGS_QUALITY, NEGATIVE_EBITDA, LEVERAGE, COVERAGE, REVENUE_DECLINE, CASH_BURN, STALE_DATA.
